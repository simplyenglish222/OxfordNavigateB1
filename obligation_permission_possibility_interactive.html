Grammar 3.2

Obligation, permission and possibility

Practise must, have to, need to, can and could.

Grammar focus

Present obligation Use must, have to and need to when something is necessary.
You must work hard to be successful.
In my job, I have to manage a team.
No necessity Use don’t have to or don’t need to when something is not necessary.
You don’t have to wear a suit.
Prohibition Use mustn’t when it is important not to do something.
You mustn’t be late for work.
Past obligation Use had to for obligation in the past.
We had to ask permission.
Past: no necessity Use didn’t have to or didn’t need to.
We didn’t have to wear a uniform.
Permission and possibility Use can and could for permission or possibility. Use can’t and couldn’t when something is not allowed or not possible.
Employees can’t make personal calls.
At school, we could choose a language.
